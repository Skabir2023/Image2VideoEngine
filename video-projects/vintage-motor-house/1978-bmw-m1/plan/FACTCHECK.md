# Fact check — 1978 BMW M1 (E26)

Angle: **Why It Failed**. Every figure below is used by the script or the caption, and every one
has a source. Anything I could not source was cut, not softened.

| Claim | Verified value | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Years built | 1978 – 1981 | BMW Group Classic, model page BMW M1 (E26) |
| Total built | **453** (399 road cars + 54 Procar racers) | BMW M1 Club, "The production of the BMW M1" (BMW production list: 460 chassis numbers assigned, 5 test chassis, 2 not built) |
| Why it existed | Homologation special for Group 4 sports-car racing | BMW Group press, "Icons for Connoisseurs — Thirty Years of the BMW M1"; Wikipedia (BMW M1) |
| The rule that mattered | Group 4 required **400** road cars built in 24 successive months | Autoconcept Reviews, BMW M1 1978 (Group 4 regulations); Wikipedia |
| The rule that broke it | Group 5 rules changed while the car was still being developed, so the planned Group 5 career never happened; the car went to Group 4 instead | Wikipedia (BMW M1); Motor Trend, "1978-1981 BMW M1" |
| Who was going to build it | Lamborghini, contracted to assemble the cars in Sant'Agata | BMW Group press; Classic Trader, "BMW M1 — Bavaria's true super sports car of the 70s" |
| What went wrong | Lamborghini's finances collapsed; BMW cancelled the contract on **20 April 1978**, after **seven** prototypes | Car and Driver, "The History of the BMW M1"; Wikipedia |
| Where it was actually built | Body (fibreglass) by TIR, Reggio Emilia; space frame by Marchesi, Modena; assembly and interior by Italdesign, Turin; final assembly by **Baur, Stuttgart**; engines and inspection by BMW Motorsport, Munich | BMW M1 Club; Hagerty, "History of the BMW M1"; 1cars.org production-chain table |
| Designer | Giorgetto Giugiaro at Italdesign, from the 1972 BMW Turbo concept | BMW Group press; BMW Group Classic |
| Engine | 3,453 cc M88 straight-six, four valves per cylinder, mid-mounted behind the cabin | BMW Group Classic |
| Power | **277 hp** (204 kW) at 6,500 rpm, 330 Nm | BMW Group Classic |
| Top speed | **262 km/h** (163 mph) works figure; 264.7 km/h measured by *auto motor und sport* in 1979 | BMW Group Classic; BMW Group press, "Icons for Connoisseurs" |
| 0 – 100 km/h | 5.6 s | BMW Group Classic |
| Price when new | **DM 100,000** — about four BMW 323is | BMW Group press, "Icons for Connoisseurs" |
| The race series it got instead | **BMW M1 Procar Championship**, 1979 and 1980, run in the build-up to European Formula 1 Grands Prix | BMW M1 Club, "Procar"; BMW M, "BMW M1: from Procar to icon" |

## Deliberately not used
- **"Only mid-engined BMW"** — the i8 is mid-engined too. Cut.
- **Horsepower of the race car** (470 hp, up to 850 hp with turbo) — real, but the numbers are all
  over the place in period sources, and the film does not need them.
- **A price in today's money** — a conversion would be invented, not sourced.
- **Any Le Mans claim.** The M1 did race at Le Mans later with privateers; "it never raced" would
  be false. The script says the planned class changed, which is what actually happened.

**0 invented facts.**
