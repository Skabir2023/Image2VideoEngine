# Script — 1978 BMW M1, "Why It Failed"

Voice: Kokoro **am_adam**, speed **1.30**, one take. Word count below is the count on the page.

## The read

BMW built this car to go racing. The rules changed before the first one was done. They needed 400 road cars to qualify, so they hired Lamborghini. Then Lamborghini ran out of cash, seven prototypes in. So every car crossed the Alps: Italian body, Italian frame, finished in Stuttgart. It still made 277 horsepower and 160 miles an hour. They built 453, then made a series where it could only race itself. The car wasn't the failure. The plan was.

## Word budget

**80 words.** For a 30 s film the page allows 78 – 90. Measured at am_adam and speed 1.30 this
paragraph reads in **25.63 s**, which puts the last word at 26.63 s and leaves 26.7 – 28.7 s of
picture for the engine roar with no voice on it.

Speed 1.30 rather than the page's 1.20 is a measurement, not a taste: this paragraph is
syllable-heavy ("Lamborghini" twice, "prototypes", "Stuttgart"), so am_adam reads it at
0.34 s/word — 27.5 s at 1.20, which would have swallowed the roar. 1.30 brings it to 0.32 s/word,
the same effective pace as the Daytona film.

## Flow test

Read as one breath: go racing → rules changed → 400 cars → hired Lamborghini → out of cash → seven
prototypes in → so the car crossed the Alps → it still made 277 and 160 → built 453 → so they made
a series → the car wasn't the failure, the plan was. Every sentence answers the one before it;
delete any and the next loses its reason to exist. The last line does not survive being said
straight after the first, which is what tells me the middle is load-bearing.
