# Eldorado Brougham — beat plan

The angle is the secret story: not the price, not the rarity alone, but the things this car
hid from everyone who was not sitting in it.

The read is one connected paragraph. Beats chain instead of closing:

1. **0.00 — the car starts.** No voice. A shut hood, a slow push, and 2.4 s of engine
   before anyone speaks. The silence is the hook: it makes the first word land.
2. **2.49 — "Seven hundred and four."** A number with no context, and no full stop after
   it — "that's how many" explains it in the same breath. The count slams to 704 at 3.40.
3. **5.90 — "and every one was finished by hand."** Cut to the profile on the word, so the
   handwork carries the same sentence forward instead of starting a new one.
4. **7.48 — "a brushed stainless roof,"** the first half of the evidence, and the comma is
   the point: the glovebox is coming, so the film does not stop here.
5. **9.05 — the bay, alone.** A 0.55 s insert with no type at all. Not a beat, a breath —
   the cut that keeps the two halves of one sentence from feeling like two films.
6. **9.60 — "a vanity set with six magnetic cups."** The interior opens on the second half
   of the evidence. This is the part people repeat.
7. **12.43 — "and then they asked $13,074."** The turn: the same story becomes money, the
   wheel holds, and "more than a Rolls-Royce" lands at 15.83 and sits.
8. **17.35 — the car leaves, and asks.** Head-on, lamps lit, the camera stepping back,
   the engine revving through the lockup — and at 17.72, out loud, "roof, or glove box,
   which would you keep?" while **THE ROOF, OR THE GLOVEBOX?** and **TELL US IN THE
   COMMENTS** come up. The engine never lifts off — the whole close is the throttle run,
   full level, and the question sits on top of it. So the film ends on the car, as it
   always did, having asked something. It ends on a choice only the viewer can make, so
   the last thing they do is type. Then black.

Camera: every plate pushes in slightly; side trucks left, rear orbits right, interior
orbits left, wheel trucks right, close pulls back — so no two consecutive cuts move the
same way. Under all of it, the same furniture keeps moving (rail, light pass, motes) so
there is never a dead frame between beats — and the right edge is left empty.
