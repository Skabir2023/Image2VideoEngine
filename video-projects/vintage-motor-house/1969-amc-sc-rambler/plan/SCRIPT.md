# Script — 1969 AMC SC/Rambler

**1969 AMC SC/Rambler: 1,512 Cars for a 500-Car Rule** · Secret Story · 55 words · voice measured after the take · film 30 s

## The read, as one paragraph

AMC never set out to build a muscle car. Racing rules said 500 identical cars. So it dropped a 315-horse 390 into a two-door Rambler. It cost $2,998. It hit 60 in 6.3. Then AMC built 1,512. That is three times what the rule demanded. And stopped. Would you take the bargain, or the badge?

One chain, not a fact list: a company with no muscle car has to satisfy a racing rule; satisfying it means a big engine in a small body; that small body is cheap and quick; the company overshoots the rule threefold and quits; the question is the choice the buyer faced. Every sentence answers the one before — “so it…”, “it cost…”, “then…”, “that is…”, “and stopped”.

Nine short sentences on purpose: each full stop is a real pause in the take, and the picture cuts in those pauses instead of on a word.

Word count: **55**. Every number is sourced in `FACTCHECK.md`: 500 identical cars for NHRA F/Stock homologation, 315 hp, 390 ci, $2,998, 0–60 6.3 s, 1,512 built. “Three times what the rule demanded” is arithmetic on the two sourced production figures, not a new claim.

## Beats

- **HOOK** — “AMC never set out to build a muscle car.” · one surprise before any number: the car was not planned.
- **SETUP** — “Racing rules said 500 identical cars.” · the rule arrives, and with it the reason the car exists at all.
- **TURN** — “So it dropped a 315-horse 390 into a two-door Rambler. It cost $2,998. It hit 60 in 6.3.” · the rule answered the cheap way: the biggest engine in the smallest body, at a compact price.
- **BIG REVELATION** — “Then AMC built 1,512. That is three times what the rule demanded.” · not a statistic: the requirement was 500 and the factory made triple it, so the homologation special was never special. The two sentences are one reveal — the number, then the meaning.
- **SATISFYING ENDING** — “And stopped. Would you take the bargain, or the badge?” · the buyer's choice, which is the argument the comments will have.

Times are measured on the new Kokoro take and copied into `scenes.py` and `audio/build_final.py` by hand; the picture cuts on those words, not on estimates. The 2.00 s lead is the engine start playing alone, so the car speaks before the narrator does, and the closing card holds while the big block answers.
