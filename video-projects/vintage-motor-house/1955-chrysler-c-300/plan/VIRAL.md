## Flow test

Read as one breath: the coupe is the most powerful → because of the Hemi → so Chrysler raced it →
it hit 127 and won the title → and then named the car after the number → would you take two gears?
Delete any sentence and the next one loses its reason. Say the last line after the first and the
middle is clearly the argument, not padding.

## Considered and rejected

- **1959 Cadillac Eldorado Biarritz — Size / Looks, 84.** The page ran a 1957 Eldorado Brougham on
  2026-11-14 and the fin story is the same story. Parked, not rejected for quality.
- **1978 Dodge Lil Red Express — Engine / Speed, 86.** The "quickest American vehicle to 100 mph in
  1978, faster than the Corvette" line is repeated everywhere but the original test number is
  contested. A hook that cannot be sourced is not a hook. Parked until the Car and Driver test is
  in hand.
- **1971 Plymouth Hemi Cuda Convertible — Price / Rarity, 83.** Values move every season, so the
  number in the hook would be stale within months.
- **1955 Chrysler 300 (this car), Size / Looks, 76.** The shape is the hook and nothing else — it
  fails the grounding test as a title. Engine / Speed is the angle that earns the car.

## Future stories — the same car, three more angles

None repeats Engine / Speed, and the 90-day lock means the car is free again on **2027-02-14**, as a
new row at the bottom of the Car List. Each hook below comes from `FACTCHECK.md`.

- **Price / Rarity — 1,725 cars at about $4,100.** "The most powerful car in America cost less than a
  Cadillac — and Chrysler still only sold 1,725 of them." The rarity is a price story, not a
  horsepower story: 33 of them left the country.
- **Secret Story — the race car that kept its leather.** "Chrysler took a New Yorker, put a race cam
  and twin four-barrels on the 331, and sold it as a luxury coupe so NASCAR would accept it." The
  homologation angle, told from the parts rather than the number.
- **Why It Became Legend — the letter series.** "Chrysler named a whole line of cars after one
  number, and it ran from 1955 to 1971." The C-300 is the first letter, which lets a Part 2 open on
  the family instead of repeating the Daytona run.
