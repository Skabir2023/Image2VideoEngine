# Fact check — 1955 Chrysler C-300

Every figure below is either spoken in the film or used in the caption. Nothing is estimated.

| Claim | Verified value | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Model year and name | 1955 Chrysler C-300, two-door hardtop coupe; the first of the "letter series" 300s | Chrysler 300 Club Inc.; Hemmings, "Year, Make and Model: 1955 Chrysler C-300" |
| Body | New Yorker hardtop with the Imperial front fascia; hardtop coupe only, three factory colours (black, Tango red, white) | Over-Drive Magazine 1955 Chrysler fact sheet; Chrysler 300 Club |
| Engine | 331.1 cu in "FirePower" Hemi V8, 300 hp at 5,200 rpm, 345 lb-ft at 3,200 | ch300imp.com factory spec table; Hemmings; Chrysler 300 Club |
| Induction and cam | Two Carter WCFB four-barrel carburettors, solid lifters, "full race" camshaft, dual exhaust | ch300imp.com; Chrysler 300 Club; Over-Drive |
| "America's most powerful car in 1955" | 300 hp was the highest rating of any US production car that year — a V-8 Corvette was 195, a Thunderbird 198, a Cadillac Eldorado 270 | Curbside Classic (Motor Life, May 1955); Collector Car Market; Hemmings |
| Daytona flying mile | 127.58 mph on the sand at Daytona Speed Weeks, February 1955 | Chrysler 300 Club Inc.; chrysler300club.com letter-car history |
| Daytona Grand National race | Averaged 92 mph; Tim Flock won on both the road course and the flying mile | Chrysler 300 Club; Hemmings |
| NASCAR title | Tim Flock took the 1955 NASCAR driver's championship in a Kiekhaefer-entered 300, winning 18 races | Hemmings, "1955 Chrysler C-300: Luxury and Performance Combined"; Hemmings Find of the Day |
| Transmission | PowerFlite two-speed automatic — the only transmission offered | Hemmings; Chrysler 300 Club; ch300imp.com |
| Production | 1,725 built, of which 33 were export cars | ch300imp.com; Chrysler 300 Club; Hemmings |
| Price | $4,055 (ch300imp.com, Chrysler 300 Club) vs $4,109 (Hemmings, twice). Difference is destination/options accounting; the caption says "about $4,100". | as listed |

## Checked and left out

- **"First 300-horsepower American car"** — repeated everywhere, but the supercharged Duesenberg SJ
  of the 1930s was rated above 300 gross hp. Both Hemmings and the 300 Club phrase it as the first
  *modern* production car at 300 hp. The film says "America's most powerful car in 1955" instead,
  which is true and does not need the qualifier.
- **0–60 in 9.8 s** (Mechanix Illustrated, Tom McCahill). Sourced, but it reads as slow to a modern
  audience and the film's point is top speed and racing, not launch. Left out of the voice.
- **"No power steering"** — Hemmings says power steering was not available; the factory option table
  at ch300imp.com lists it at $113. The sources conflict, so the film does not claim it.
- **130 mph** (McCahill's flat-out figure) conflicts with the 127.58 mph record run; the film uses
  the record.
- **"18 races"** is Flock's 1955 win count in the 300, not the number of 300 wins by every driver.
  The voice does not say it; the caption does, in Flock's name.
- Auction values change every season, so no current price is quoted. The caption's "$4,100" is the
  1955 list price, not a value today.

## Verdict

**0 invented facts.** The spoken numbers are four: 1955, 300 horsepower, 331 cubic inches, 127 mph,
plus the NASCAR title, all traceable to the rows above. "Two gears" is the PowerFlite's specced
ratio count, not a nickname. The caption repeats only those figures and adds production (1,725) and
Flock's 18 wins, both sourced here.
