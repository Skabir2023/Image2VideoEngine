# Fact-check — 1969 Dodge Charger Daytona

Every claim in the voice and on the screen, and where it stands. Angle: **Secret Story**.

| # | Claim | Verdict | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Dodge built **503** Charger Daytonas | Verified | Hemmings, *1969 Dodge Charger Daytona*; Old Cars Weekly; Wikipedia — all 503 |
| 2 | NASCAR required **500** cars sold to the public for homologation | Verified | FOX Sports; OnAllCylinders; Sports Car Digest |
| 3 | The rear wing stood **23 inches** (584 mm) high, on struts | Verified | Wikipedia (584 mm / 23 in); Hemmings (23.5 in tall); Street Muscle |
| 4 | The wing was mounted high to reach **clean air** above the roof — not to clear the trunk lid | Verified | OnAllCylinders states the trunk-lid story is a rumour and the height was for clean air |
| 5 | Pointed **nose cone** (18 in) and **flush rear window** | Verified | Hemmings; Street Muscle; Wikipedia |
| 6 | The shape reached **200 mph in 1970** (Buddy Baker, 200.447 mph, Talladega, March 1970) | Verified | Hemmings — "Buddy Baker set a 200.447 mph record in March 1970 at Talladega" |

## Claims deliberately left out
- Horsepower. The 440 Magnum and the optional 426 Hemi both have numbers, but the film's
  story is the loophole and the wing; a third figure would crowd a 30 s read.
- "Only 70 Hemis" — true, but it needs a second sentence to land, and the read has no room.

## Notes
- The script says "200 miles per hour", not "a 200 mph lap", so it does not claim the run was
  a race lap. The record run was a closed-course test.
- No invented numbers, no "reportedly", no price.
