# Fact check — 1977 Oldsmobile Cutlass Supreme

The brief was a shocking story or an untruthful claim by a car company. This is the clearest
documented case in the list: a division advertised a "Rocket V8" and, without telling buyers,
installed a Chevrolet V8 instead — with a federal jury finding for the owners.

Every figure below is either spoken in the film or used in the caption. Nothing is estimated.

## The line the film walks

The swap was **not** a secret conspiracy at the top of GM — engine sharing between divisions was
decades-old practice, and the Chevrolet 350 was a good engine. What was untrue was the **claim**:
Oldsmobile kept advertising the Rocket V8 while shipping a Chevrolet in its place, and the
corporation then repainted every division's engines one colour, which removed the one visual tell.
The film says exactly that and nothing stronger. It does not say every 1977 Oldsmobile had a
Chevrolet engine — the class covered those that did, and the film's line is "buyers ordered an
Oldsmobile Rocket 350 … mechanics found a Chevy".

## Checked and left out

- **"128,000 cars"** — that is the press's estimate of GM's exposure if every buyer took its
  buy-back offer (3.7% of 1977-model production), not the number of cars built with the wrong
  engine. Not used.
- **"$200 settlement"** — real, but it was rejected on appeal before the 1981 verdict, so quoting it
  as the outcome would be wrong. The film says "four years later … 550 dollars".
- **"Chevy engines were worse"** — the sources disagree; hot rodders thought the Chevrolet
  small-block was the better engine. The film never claims it was worse, only that it was not what
  was sold.
- **The 1981 figure as "$7.7 million"** — spoken as "fourteen thousand owners got 550 dollars
  each", which is the jury's own arithmetic; the total is in the caption. Mac's "$8 million+" is the
  final cost including fees, and the caption does not need it.
- **Cutlass Supreme production split by engine** — not published, so the film never says how many
  Cutlasses specifically got the Chevrolet. The class definition and the Delta 88 share carry the
  scale.

## Verdict

**0 invented facts.** Spoken numbers: 1977, a 350 Rocket, $550 each, 14,000 owners, four years.
All five are in the table above, and the one claim that could be over-read — "General Motors
painted every engine blue so nobody could tell" — is the documented 1977 corporate-blue change and
is scoped to that model year.
