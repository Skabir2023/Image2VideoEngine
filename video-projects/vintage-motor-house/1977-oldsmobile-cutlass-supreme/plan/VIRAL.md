# Viral score — 1977 Oldsmobile Cutlass Supreme

**Hook:** "In 1977, America's best-selling car was hiding a Chevrolet."

**Brief (the person's words):** a shocking story or an untruthful claim by a car company. This is
the clearest sourced one on the list — a division advertised its own "Rocket V8", shipped Chevrolet
engines instead, repainted every division's engine the same colour, and lost in federal court.

**Angle:** Secret Story. The page's last film (1955 Chrysler C-300) ran Engine / Speed, and the two
before it ran Secret Story and Engine / Speed on different cars; this car has never been filmed, so
the pair is new and the angle is not two in a row on the same subject.

## Flow test

Read as one breath: the best-selling car hides a Chevrolet → because buyers ordered a Rocket → and
the mechanic found a Chevy, the belt would not fit → and GM painted them all blue so you could not
tell → four years later 14,000 owners were paid → would you have looked at yours? Delete any
sentence and the next loses its reason: without the order sheet there is no deception, without the
fan belt there is no discovery, without the paint there is no cover-up, without the payout there is
no ending. Say the last line straight after the first and the middle is plainly the proof, not
padding.

## Considered and rejected

- **1970 Chevrolet Chevelle SS 454 — Engine / Speed, 86.** The "450 hp was really 500" story is
  repeated everywhere and every dyno figure is a guess dressed as a fact. A hook that cannot be
  sourced is not a hook.
- **1960 Chevrolet Corvair — Secret Story, 89.** GM's detectives investigating Ralph Nader is a
  genuinely shocking story, but it is about a man, not an untruthful claim about the car, and the
  Corvair's handling is still argued. Parked for a later batch with a different framing.
- **1971 Chevrolet Vega — Why It Failed, 82.** The sleeveless aluminium block is a real failure, but
  "untruthful claim" is the wrong frame: Chevrolet believed the engine, it did not lie about it.
- **1957 Chrysler 300C — Size / Looks, 79.** Already parked in the C-300 plan and still below 80.
- **1982 Cadillac Cimarron — Why It Failed, 85.** A strong story ("a Cavalier with a Cadillac
  price"), but the deception is pricing and trim, which reads as opinion; the engine swap has a
  court verdict behind it and therefore a harder number.

## Future stories — the same car, three more angles

None repeats Secret Story, and the 90-day lock makes the car free again on **2027-02-16**, as a new
row at the bottom of the Car List.

- **Size / Looks — 632,742 cars.** "Oldsmobile built 632,742 Cutlasses in 1977 and passed a million
  cars for the first time — on the strength of one coupe." A sales story, not an engine story.
- **Price / Rarity — the Supreme coupe at $4,670.** "The best-selling car in America cost less than
  a Caprice and outsold it." Dollar figures and a trim walk.
- **Why It Became Legend — the Colonnade body.** "Five years of the same body, and it still sold
  better every year." The design story, and the last of the big intermediates.
- **Engine / Speed — the 403.** "The Cutlass you wanted had 185 hp, and it was the engine nobody
  talks about." Rotates onto the engine, but only after the 90 days and never as a repeat of the
  swap.

## Decision

**93 ≥ 80. Build the 1977 Oldsmobile Cutlass Supreme on Secret Story.** Car List row 182
(`No. 182`), `Used? = No` until the film is finished.
