# Content rules — binding, from MASTER PROMPT.md

> Part of the engine manual. Index, read order and precedence: [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md).

- **Viral score before the script.** Seven spare lines: hook, rubric score out of 100 (hook shock
  25, relatability 20, curiosity gap 20, shareability 15, visual wow 20). **Below 80 is rejected**
  — show the rejected topics and why, pick another car.
- **No invented facts.** Year, make/model/trim, engine size, production number, and any price or
  speed claim must be verified; if unsure, cut the claim. "Reportedly / around" is allowed, a
  made-up number is not. Horsepower may simply be left out (the reference build does).
- **Research the car before writing.** Why it was built, what it was meant to beat, its real price
  and production, its engineering and racing history, and why it became legendary, forgotten,
  controversial or a failure. Use manufacturer documents, period publications, auction records and
  reputable historians. When a claim is disputed, say so; never present one version as settled.
- **The story must be complete.** Never end on "But the real secret was…", "However, nobody
  knew…", "The truth was even more surprising…" or "And that's where things get interesting…"
  unless the next sentence actually delivers it. No artificial cliffhanger, no cut-off last
  sentence. A complete 20-second story beats an incomplete 30-second one.
- **Angle bank.** The brain may use the 15 story directions in `MASTER PROMPT.md`, but it records
  one of the page's six angles in the Tracker. Rotate them across a batch and never repeat a car +
  angle pair; the 90-day return is [Choosing the next car](14-choosing-the-next-car.md).
- **Script length:** 30 s is the page default. 30–45 words for 15–20 s, 60–90 for 30 s, 90–135 for
  45 s, 120–180 for 60 s. Count them, measure the take, and never pad a thin story or cut a
  complete one to hit a number.
- **The script is one chain, not a list.** Every sentence grows out of the one before it — carry a
  word forward, answer the question the last line opened ("So they…", "But it…", "That meant…"),
  or raise the stakes. No "Also" / "Another thing", no fact dropped in from a new subject, no two
  sentences that could swap places. The four beats (setup, turn, proof, payoff) are links, not
  sections. Run the flow test on every script: read it aloud as one breath, delete each sentence
  and check the next still works (if it does, that sentence was a detour), say the last line
  straight after the first (if it still makes sense, the middle was padding). Tone is an excited
  teacher who just found this out — every number lands as a surprise the previous line set up, and
  the viewer ends feeling they learned something they can repeat.
- **One plate per 3 s of film, every plate a different image.** The six fixed angles come first —
  front ¾ hero, side profile, rear, engine bay, interior, wheel — and no file is used twice. **No
  humans, no animals, no reflections of people, no text, no watermark**, car only, same colour,
  wheels, tyres and body style in every plate. A period advertisement, press photograph or
  archival race shot is a legal plate when it is genuinely this car. The cadence, the close-ups
  and the route: [Plates](03-working-procedure.md#4-plates--one-still-per-3-seconds).
- Caption is **exactly three parts, no labels** (title / description / hashtags), blank line
  between each:
  - **title** = year + make + model + one power word, under 12 words, no full stop; it is also the
    file name.
  - **description** = **a real paragraph, 3–5 sentences, 45–80 words** — never a one-liner. Open
    on the hook, spend the middle on the verified numbers the film used, talk to the viewer in the
    second person, compare the car to something they know, and close on a call to action: a
    question they can answer in a comment. No invented facts, no emoji, no links, no hashtags
    inside it.
  - **hashtags** = **4–5**, one line starting with a single space: one brand tag + one classic-car
    tag + one model or year tag (+ one more only if it is specific to this car). Never
    `#viral #fyp #reels #trending #foryou`.
- **The caption is scored, not just formatted.** 80 or more on the same five weights as the car,
  and the title has to pass the grounding test — the pass, the gate and the rewrite rule are [The
  caption pass](05-the-caption-pass.md), run before step 6.
- American English, short sentences, no emoji, no "hey guys", no hashtags in the voice.
